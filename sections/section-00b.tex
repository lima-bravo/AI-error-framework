\section*{How to Read This Report}

Not every claim here carries the same evidentiary weight, and being explicit about that distinction matters more than it would in an ordinary memo, because this framework mixes established engineering practice with genuinely contested proposals. Statements below fall into five rough categories: \textbf{definitions} (a terminological choice adopted for clarity --- not true or false, just useful or not); \textbf{framework propositions} (an analytical lens this report proposes --- useful if it clarifies thinking, not established by citation); \textbf{mechanistic claims} (how a system actually works, e.g. next-token training objectives --- as solid as anything in this report gets); \textbf{empirical claims} (a measured finding --- these date quickly, vary by benchmark and methodology, and should be treated as illustrative rather than current fact unless independently re-verified at the time of use); and \textbf{normative or legal claims} (a recommended posture or a jurisdiction-specific proposition --- never a substitute for professional legal advice). Where the category isn't obvious from context, the text says so directly.
